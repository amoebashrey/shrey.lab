# shrey.lab — Voice & Copywriting Guide

> The voice rubric for everything written under shrey.lab — microcopy, blog posts, case studies, social posts, emails. Use this whenever you need to write or edit content. Pairs with BRAND.md but stands alone.

---

## The voice in one paragraph

A slightly nerdy, well-dressed late-twenty-something on a clean metro — confident, carefree but intentional, knows their value, doesn't perform it. That's the voice. **Quiet authority. No hard sell.** Dry humor that surfaces in microcopy and footers, never in headlines. Assume the reader is smart.

---

## The four dials

Every piece of writing balances these four. shrey.lab sits in a specific place on each.

| Dial | Position | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Funny ↔ Serious | **70% serious, 30% dry-funny** | Humor lives in 404s, empty states, footer signatures, tooltips. Never in value propositions, hero copy, or case study summaries. |
| Formal ↔ Casual | **Casual but precise** | Contractions yes ("I'm," "won't," "it's"). Slang sparingly. "We" and "I" both work. No corporate-speak. |
| Respectful ↔ Irreverent | **Respectful, with aim** | Irreverent only toward bad UX, big-corp websites, template aesthetics, and other deserving targets. Never toward people. |
| Enthusiastic ↔ Matter-of-fact | **Matter-of-fact** | The work speaks. We don't sell. No "amazing," "incredible," excessive exclamation points. State what it is. |

---

## The five rules

1. **Lead with the noun, not the adjective.** "A productivity app" beats "A revolutionary productivity app." Start with what the thing *is*.
2. **Active voice. Short sentences. Then a longer one for rhythm.**
3. **No empty intensifiers.** Cut "really," "very," "truly," "literally." If a sentence needs them to land, the sentence needs rewriting.
4. **No buzzwords.** "Synergy," "leverage," "10x," "game-changing," "AI-powered," "revolutionary" — banned.
5. **Specifics beat generics.** "A Slack bot that nudges you about your top three priorities daily" beats "An AI productivity assistant." Always.

---

## Words to use vs. avoid

| ✓ Use | ✗ Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Built, shipped, made | Crafted, curated | The latter pair are overused into meaninglessness |
| Tool, utility, app | Solution, platform | Concrete vs. corporate |
| Work, project, case study | Portfolio piece | "Portfolio piece" sounds like a deliverable to a teacher |
| Get in touch, reach out | Connect, sync | Sync is a meeting; connect is LinkedIn |
| I, we | The team (when there's no team) | Don't pretend to be bigger |
| Useful, considered, opinionated | Innovative, cutting-edge | Useful is the highest compliment in this brand |
| Ship, launch, release | Roll out, deploy (in marketing copy) | Deploy works for engineering. Not marketing. |
| Stop, fix, kill | Sunset, deprecate (in casual contexts) | Sunset is fine in technical docs; not in blogs |

## Microcopy patterns (the tone in action)

These are the canonical examples. When in doubt, rewrite to land somewhere in this register.

### 404 pages
> Well, this is awkward. The page you're looking for either never existed or quietly retired. Want to head back?

> Looks like this page isn't live yet, or I broke something while building it (very possible). Head back and try again?

> This page doesn't exist anymore. Or never did. Either way — let's find you something that does.

### Empty states
- "Nothing here yet. Worth fixing."
- "No results — your search was either very specific or I haven't built that yet."
- "Empty inbox. Suspiciously calm."

### Form submissions
- "Got it. I'll be in touch — usually within a day or two."
- "Thanks. I read every one."

### Error toasts
- "Something broke on my end. Try again, and if it keeps happening, tell me."
- "That didn't work. Not your fault."

### Loading states
- Short waits: "Thinking..."
- Longer waits: "Doing the thing."
- Slack-bot specific: "On it."

### Success toasts
- "Done."
- "Saved."
- "Sent. (You can close this.)"

### Confirmation / destructive actions
- "Delete this? Can't undo."
- "Are you sure? This goes nowhere good."

### Coming-soon / waitlist
- "Not ready yet. Drop your email and I'll tell you when it is."

## Long-form writing (blog posts, case studies)

### Structure

A shrey.lab blog post or case study follows this rough shape:

1. **Hook** — a specific observation, story, or question. Not a thesis statement. ("Last Tuesday I shipped a Slack bot that turned out to be useless. Here's why.")
2. **Setup** — the context, what you were trying to do, why it mattered.
3. **The middle** — what happened. Specific. Concrete. Use real numbers, real names (with permission), real screenshots.
4. **The turn** — the insight, the lesson, the thing that surprised you.
5. **The close** — a single sentence that lands the post. Not a summary. A line.

### Headlines

- Concrete and specific. "How I built a Slack bot that nudges, doesn't nag" beats "Building better Slack bots."
- One italic word in Instrument Serif inside the headline if it has rhythm — "Built for *shipping* fast."
- Sentence case, not title case. Capitalize the first letter and proper nouns. That's it.
- If it ends in a question mark, it should actually be a question.

### Subheads

- Short. Often a phrase, not a sentence. "The first version was broken." "Why most Slack bots fail."
- Use them to mark turns in the argument, not to recap.

### Paragraph length

- Mix it up. One-line paragraph for emphasis. Three or four lines for body. Five is the ceiling — if it's longer, break it.
- Single-sentence paragraphs are encouraged.
- Like this.

### Quotes

- When you quote yourself or others, use Instrument Serif italic for emphasis pulls. Standard quotation marks otherwise.

### Lists

- Bulleted lists: only when the items genuinely need to be parallel. Otherwise, write prose.
- Numbered lists: for actual sequences. Steps, ranks, ordered timelines.
- Don't use lists to summarize what could be a paragraph.

### Stats and numbers

- Numbers below 10: spell out ("three teams," "four months").
- Numbers 10+: digits ("14 teams," "30L batches").
- Percentages: digits + % ("20%").
- Money: with currency ("₹35,000," "$200/month").
- Time periods in headlines: shortest form ("90 days," "3 months").

## Email & messaging

### Cold outreach (e.g. to a hiring manager)

> Hey {name},
>
> {one-line specific reason this person, this company} — I noticed {specific, concrete observation showing you did 5 minutes of research}.
>
> I'm a product builder; I shipped {one specific, recent thing — not a list}. I think there's an interesting fit with {what they're working on}.
>
> If a 15-min call this week or next makes sense, here's my calendar: {link}. If not, no worries.
>
> – Shreyas

- **Never** start with "I hope this email finds you well."
- **Never** introduce yourself as "I'm a product manager / product builder with X years of experience." Show, don't tell.
- One ask per email. Not three.

### Follow-ups

> Hey {name} — bumping this in case it got buried.
>
> If now's not a good time, just say so and I'll stop bothering you.

That's it. No "just circling back to see if you had a chance to consider..."

### Replies

- Match the formality of the incoming message. If they wrote "Hi Shreyas," reply "Hi {name}." If they wrote "shreyas — quick q —" reply in kind.
- Get to the point in the first line. Pleasantries can come second.

## Social copy (LinkedIn, Twitter/X, threads)

### LinkedIn posts

- First line is a hook. Second line is an empty line (creates the "see more" cliff in the feed).
- No hashtags in the body. Three at the end, max. Lowercase.
- No "agree?" or "thoughts?" at the end. Cringe.
- End with either: a question that's actually interesting, an observation that lands, or nothing at all.
- Length: ~150 words is the sweet spot. Threads work at 300+.

### Twitter/X

- One idea per tweet. Don't cram.
- Threads are fine when a single tweet can't carry the idea. Number them only if order matters.
- Reply guy energy: low. Don't dunk on people.
- Don't subtweet. If something deserves a critique, name it.

## Things to never do

- ❌ Start anything with "In today's fast-paced world..."
- ❌ Use "literally" unless you mean it literally.
- ❌ Use "the only X that..." — almost always wrong, almost always weak.
- ❌ End a piece with "What do you think?" / "Thoughts?" / "Let me know in the comments." Lazy.
- ❌ Brag without evidence. ("I'm an expert in..." vs. "I shipped X to Y users in Z months.")
- ❌ Apologize for the post existing. ("Sorry for the long post but...")
- ❌ Use ChatGPT-style transitions: "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally." Cut them or say "also."
- ❌ Use ChatGPT-style tells: em-dashes everywhere, "delve into," "tapestry of," "navigate the landscape of." If a sentence sounds like an LLM wrote it, rewrite it.
- ❌ Compliment-sandwich criticism. If something's broken, say it's broken.
- ❌ Hashtag-stuff. Three is plenty.
- ❌ Use emojis as bullet points. ✅ ❌ ✨ — sparingly, with intent, never as decoration.

## A self-edit checklist

Before publishing anything more than a tweet, run through this list:

1. Can I cut the first line? (Often the real start is the second line.)
2. Are there any words ending in "-ly" I can delete? (Usually yes.)
3. Did I use any banned words? (Search for them.)
4. Does the headline lead with the noun?
5. Is there at least one specific, concrete detail in the first paragraph?
6. Did I close with a line that lands, or did I just stop?
7. Read it aloud. Does any sentence make me trip? Rewrite that one.
8. If it's longer than 500 words, can I cut 20%? (Always, yes.)

## Using this with AI

When asking Claude or any AI to write in shrey.lab voice, include this in the prompt:

> Write this in shrey.lab voice. Read VOICE.md for the rules. Key constraints: quiet authority, no hard sell, lead with the noun, active voice, no buzzwords, no empty intensifiers, dry humor only in microcopy never in value props. Reference the microcopy patterns for tone calibration. If a sentence sounds like a generic LLM wrote it, rewrite it.
